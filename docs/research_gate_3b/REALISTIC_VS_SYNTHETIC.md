# Realistic vs synthetic attributes

| Attribute | EVRP-TW-D | LaDe | Amazon LMRC | ESOGU-ML5EV | Schneider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Road geometry | **Metadata:** OSM/TIGER; **archive:** 2-norm in `[0,100]^2` only | City road nets available separately | Obfuscated stop graphs + travel times | ~2 km instrumented loop | Euclidean |
| Customers / stops | Geography-seeded; daily activation synthetic | Real packages | Real historical stops | N/A | Synthetic benchmark |
| Time windows | Generated by benchmark pipeline | Accept/delivery event times (observed) | Package windows in JSON | N/A | Benchmark windows |
| Charging stations | Real AFDC candidates | None (not EV) | None (not EV) | Charging flags on test EV | Synthetic stations |
| Energy / SOC | Not measured on routes; must be modeled | Not EV | Not EV | Measured SOC / currents | Linear `r * distance` |
| Online release process | Semi-synthetic operating days | Accept timestamps usable as release proxies | Route already committed historically | N/A | Scenarioized release |

## Combining demand and energy datasets

**Can combine (with explicit assumptions):**

- EVRP-TW-D geography + stations + versioned vehicle configs calibrated for order-of-magnitude against ESOGU consumption.
- LaDe accept times as a **non-EV** template for request-arrival processes overlaid on a territory (synthetic mapping).

**Must not combine naively:**

- Do not assign ESOGU SOC traces to Amazon/LaDe couriers.
- Do not treat LaDe `delivery_time` as known at dispatch.
- Do not replace EVRP-TW-D road distances with Euclidean hypot.
- Do not claim measured battery feasibility on Amazon routes without an energy model.

## Implication

The scientifically honest flagship is **EVRP-TW-D + explicit vehicle/energy model (+ optional LaDe-style arrival process)**. Schneider remains a unit-test oracle for algorithms, not the application claim.
